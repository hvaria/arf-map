# Phase 1 — Admin Discovery (Simulated CA RCFE Administrator)

> **Status:** DRAFT — awaiting Phase 1 gate review.
> **Source:** Synthesized from publicly available CDSS Community Care
> Licensing Division (CCLD) materials, Title 22 Division 6 Chapter 8
> (Residential Care Facilities for the Elderly), and common operational
> patterns at small-to-mid-size RCFE / ARF facilities (6–49 beds).
> **Caveat:** This is a *simulation*, not a transcript of a real admin.
> Every regulation reference, retention period, frequency, and form
> number is marked `[V]` (needs validation) below and aggregated in
> §11 Open Questions. Treat the narrative as a starting hypothesis, not
> the law.

## 0. Persona summary

- **Role:** Licensed RCFE Administrator (also doubles as DON for many
  small facilities). Holds the CA RCFE Administrator Certificate.
- **Facility size:** 6–25 beds (modal segment for this product, per
  facility table content; ARFs trend smaller, RCFEs span 6→100+ but the
  long tail is 6–15 bed board-and-care homes).
- **Tech comfort:** Low to moderate. Uses email, WhatsApp, and a printer
  daily. Lives in spreadsheets and 3-ring binders. Has tried point
  solutions (paper MAR, Excel staff schedule) and abandoned them when
  the upkeep cost exceeded the benefit.
- **Primary stress drivers** (verbatim phrasing the admin would use):
  - "I never know if I'm inspection-ready until I'm digging through binders the night before."
  - "I have to remember everybody's TB renewal date in my head."
  - "When something happens at 2 a.m. I'm the one the state asks for paperwork."
  - "Half my staff don't read English well, and the other half don't read regulations at all."
  - "Every time we hire someone new I rebuild the same 14-page packet from scratch."

## 1. Daily operations narrative

### Morning (arrival → 10:00)
1. **Overnight log review.** Read the NOC-shift narrative log. Look
   for: falls, behavior incidents, refusals, vital-sign anomalies,
   bowel/bladder issues, hospital sends, family calls. Anything
   abnormal escalates.
2. **Resident census reconciliation.** Confirm who is physically in the
   building vs. the roster. Out-on-pass, hospitalized, deceased,
   discharged — each has its own paperwork trail.
3. **Shift handoff observation.** NOC → AM verbal handoff. Admin signs
   off that key items (medications due in next hour, doctor's visits,
   transport pickups) are acknowledged by the AM lead.
4. **MAR sweep (last 24 h).** Walk the Medication Administration Record
   for any missed (M), refused (R), or held (H) doses. Missed doses are
   the single most common deficiency cited on inspection. `[V]`
5. **Staff arrivals.** Confirm all scheduled staff clocked in.
   Identify call-outs. Trigger replacement coverage. Document the
   reason (call-out, family emergency, etc.). Call-out documentation
   matters because chronic understaffing is a citation pattern. `[V]`
6. **Breakfast oversight.** Watch the dining room. Confirm special
   diets honored (diabetic, pureed, thickened liquids — choking risk
   is the #1 acute-care liability). Cross-check against current diet
   orders.
7. **Family communications.** Return overnight messages.
   Document every clinical conversation in the resident's record
   ("daughter called re: pain management, agreed to call MD" —
   undocumented family calls become "you never told me" complaints).

### Midday (10:00 → 14:00)
8. **Provider visits / transport.** Doctor visits, hospice nurse,
   home health, podiatrist, hairdresser. Each non-staff visitor must
   sign in. Any provider giving care leaves a visit note that must be
   filed in the resident chart same day.
9. **Move-ins.** Pre-admission appraisal (LIC 603 family) `[V]`,
   physician's report (LIC 602) within 30 days `[V]`, TB clearance
   (resident, not just staff) `[V]`, admission agreement (LIC 604)
   `[V]`, personal rights acknowledgment, advance directive, POLST if
   applicable, medication intake, valuables inventory, photograph for
   chart and emergency card.
10. **Move-outs / discharges.** 30-day notice unless emergency, exit
    appraisal, return of personal property, refund calculation, final
    medication accounting (controlled substances are reconciled and
    witnessed by two staff `[V]`).
11. **Pre-audit binder check-ins (on inspection-anxious days).** Pull
    representative resident charts and verify they have a current
    physician's report, signed appraisal, signed admission agreement,
    medication list matching MAR, and rights acknowledgment.

### Afternoon (14:00 → 18:00)
12. **Activity program oversight.** Activity calendar posted in
    common area. Activities documented as attended/refused per
    resident. Used during inspections to evidence quality-of-life
    programming.
13. **Maintenance & environment.** Walk the building: hot water temp at
    a resident-accessible faucet (≤110°F per Title 22 §87303(g) `[V]`),
    refrigerator/freezer temps logged, smoke detector status,
    grab bars secure, exit doors unobstructed, hazardous chemicals
    locked.
14. **PM-shift handoff.** Same protocol as AM; admin often present to
    bridge the day's issues forward.

### Evening (18:00 → end of day)
15. **MAR finalization for daytime doses.** Confirm noon and 4 PM
    medications passed and signed. Any held doses noted with reason
    (e.g., "BP 95/60, held metoprolol per PRN parameter").
16. **End-of-day note.** Admin writes a brief shift-summary entry —
    census, incidents, staffing notes, follow-ups for tomorrow.
17. **Family follow-ups.** Promised callbacks before EOD; documented.
18. **Tomorrow's prep.** Anyone with an outside appointment, transport
    confirmed, meds prepared to travel (with documentation), DPOA
    notified.

### Always-on (any hour)
- **Incident response.** Fall, elopement attempt, hospital transfer,
  injury, death, abuse allegation. Admin notifies CCLD by phone within
  the regulated window `[V]` and files LIC 624 (Unusual Incident
  Report) on the required form `[V]`. Family/responsible party
  contacted same day. Internal post-incident huddle within 24 h.
- **Complaint intake.** Resident, family, staff, neighbor, or
  Ombudsman complaint. Logged in complaint file with date, complainant,
  nature, investigation steps, resolution, retention. `[V]`

## 2. Weekly cadence

| Day | Recurring obligation |
|---|---|
| Mon | Build next week's staff schedule; confirm shift coverage; check upcoming staff certification expirations (TB, CPR, First Aid, fingerprint) `[V]` |
| Tue | Petty cash / resident trust account reconciliation `[V]`; vendor invoice review (food, medical supplies, pharmacy) |
| Wed | Maintenance walk + log; environmental temperature logs reviewed; bath/shower roster confirmed against weekly minimum `[V]` |
| Thu | Resident weight checks `[V]`; chart audit on a rolling sample (e.g., 2 charts per week so every chart is audited within the quarter) |
| Fri | Activity program attendance review; family newsletter / communication batch; payroll prep |
| Sat | Census reconciliation; emergency contact list updated |
| Sun | Lighter day; admin frequently uses for catch-up paperwork and the next week's planning |

**Notes that recur weekly but aren't day-specific:**
- Fire drill / disaster drill if the quarter's quota hasn't been met
  (RCFEs require drills at stated intervals across shifts) `[V]`.
- Staff in-service mini-training (15–30 min topic).
- Pest control / housekeeping vendor visits.
- Trash, laundry, kitchen deep-clean rotation.

## 3. Monthly cadence

- **Resident chart audits** — sample chosen so every resident chart is
  audited at least once per quarter. Looks for: current physician's
  report, current appraisal, MAR completeness, missing signatures,
  rights/advance directive in file, photo, dietary order, allergies,
  DNR status, code status, hospice election if applicable.
- **MAR audit** — sample doses cross-checked against pharmacy fills,
  refusal/missed-dose trend by resident and by shift.
- **Controlled substance inventory** — count and reconcile narcotics,
  log discrepancies, two-staff witness signature. `[V]`
- **Staff training compliance** — confirm everyone's annual in-service
  hours on pace `[V]`; identify expiring certifications in next 60–90
  days (TB, CPR/First Aid, dementia care, food handler, fingerprint).
- **Policies & posting compliance** — required postings present and
  legible (license, residents' rights, complaint info, ombudsman
  number, evacuation map, hot water reminder, fire safety, no smoking).
  `[V]`
- **Fire drill** — must occur at frequencies/shift coverage prescribed
  by regulation; logged with date, time, shift, evacuation duration,
  participants. `[V]`
- **Maintenance log** — preventive maintenance items (filters, smoke
  detector batteries, generator test if any). `[V]`
- **Census + billing reconciliation** — private-pay rates, third-party
  payor remittances, SSI / In-Home Supportive Services if applicable,
  level-of-care surcharges. Discrepancies escalate to the owner.
- **Resident council** if facility runs one — minutes filed.
- **Insurance certificates of vendors** — verify current.

## 4. Quarterly cadence

- **Disaster drill** at higher intensity than a fire drill — table-top
  or simulated, covers shelter-in-place, evacuation rally point,
  utility loss, communication tree, family notification protocol. `[V]`
- **Comprehensive policy review** — admin signs that policies were
  reviewed; revisions if regs changed.
- **Equipment safety check** — mechanical lift, transfer aids,
  shower chairs, wheelchairs, oxygen concentrators (if licensed for
  it), AED battery & pads expiration.
- **Resident chart audit cycle close** — every chart audited at least
  once.
- **Staff competency reassessment** — direct observation of med pass,
  transfers, hand hygiene, glove use, infection control.
- **Annual-readiness checkpoint** — at Q3, admin runs an internal
  "are we license-renewal-ready" sweep so the Q4 renewal isn't a fire
  drill.

## 5. Annual cadence

- **License renewal** — RCFE license renewal application, fee, updated
  emergency disaster plan, current rosters, current physician's reports
  for all residents, current staff fingerprint clearances and TB tests,
  required postings, plan of operations if amended. `[V]`
- **Liability insurance renewal** — general liability + professional
  liability. Certificate filed.
- **Fire marshal inspection** — annual (or per AHJ cycle) extinguisher
  service, alarm panel test, sprinkler inspection if applicable. `[V]`
- **Health Department food permit / inspection** if the facility
  prepares meals on-site. `[V]`
- **Staff TB renewal** (annually after initial within first 7 days of
  hire). `[V]`
- **Fingerprint clearance** — most are continuous via DOJ subsequent
  arrest notification (Live Scan once at hire), but the facility must
  maintain documentation that the clearance is on file. `[V]`
- **CPR / First Aid renewal** for required staff — typically 2-year
  cycle per cert; admin tracks expiration. `[V]`
- **Administrator continuing education** — admin must complete a set
  number of CE hours per cert renewal cycle. `[V]`
- **Annual resident physical** — most residents need an annual
  physician's report on file; some conditions require more often. `[V]`
- **Annual chart review** — comprehensive vs. monthly's sampling.
- **Disaster plan review & revision** — re-publish; staff resign
  acknowledgment.
- **Resident agreement annual review** — rate changes, service
  changes, notice requirements per LIC 604 `[V]`.
- **Hood / grease trap / vent hood cleaning** if applicable. `[V]`
- **Resident satisfaction survey** (best practice, not necessarily
  required `[V]`).

## 8. Audit & inspection touchpoints

### Who shows up

- **CDSS Community Care Licensing Division (CCLD)** — annual or
  unannounced, plus complaint follow-up. `[V]` Annual inspection
  cycle was on a 5-year reduced frequency, then changed; current
  cadence must be confirmed.
- **Long-Term Care Ombudsman** — periodic visits, complaint follow-up.
- **Local fire marshal / AHJ** — fire/life safety.
- **Local health department** — food service (if applicable), some
  jurisdictions inspect facility-wide.
- **State Fire Marshal** for >6-bed facilities `[V]`.
- **Insurance / risk audits** — carrier or broker walk-through.
- **Owner / corporate audits** — internal; common in multi-facility
  operators.

### Typical inspection narrative

1. Auditor arrives; admin shows license, current roster, staffing list
   for today.
2. **Tour:** kitchen, med room, resident rooms (selected), bathrooms,
   exits, fire panel, emergency supplies, postings.
3. **Records sample:**
   - "Pick me three residents at random." Pulls full chart.
   - "Pick me three staff hired in the last 12 months." Pulls
     personnel file.
   - Last 60 days of MAR for one resident.
   - Last 6 months of fire drills.
   - Last 12 months of temp logs (fridge, hot water).
   - Recent incident reports + state notifications.
   - Last disaster drill.
   - Current training matrix.
   - Resident trust reconciliation if any private-pay residents have
     funds in custody.
4. **Interviews:** admin, 1–2 staff, 1–2 residents, family if present.
5. **Exit interview:** preliminary findings discussed. Citations
   issued on inspection report; correction plans required with due
   dates.

### What separates a clean inspection from a citation

- Documentation that is *fresh, dated, signed, complete*.
- Postings visible and current.
- Staff who can name the abuse reporting hotline and the ombudsman.
- Residents who can describe the complaint process.
- No backdating, no white-out, no missing signatures.
- Temperature logs without obvious copy-paste pattern.
- Incident reports with closed-loop evidence (state notified,
  family notified, internal review held, corrective action listed).

## 9. Pain points, failure modes, and fire drills

### Top operational pain points (in admin's own language)

1. **"Renewal whack-a-mole."** Every staff member has 4–6 expiring
   things on different cycles. The admin keeps a paper spreadsheet,
   forgets to update it, then a TB lapses and the staff member shows
   up to a shift.
2. **"Pre-audit paper hunt."** 24–72 hours before an inspection, the
   admin pulls all-nighters compiling proof. The information exists
   but is scattered across binders, MAR sheets, the cook's clipboard,
   and personal notes.
3. **"State called and I had 10 minutes."** Phone calls from a
   licensing analyst asking for a specific record. No way to pull a
   sample chart, MAR, or drill log without physically walking the
   building.
4. **"Did we close that out?"** Incidents have a 7-stage life cycle
   (occurrence, immediate response, CCLD verbal notification, family
   notification, written report, internal review, corrective action,
   follow-up). Closing the loop is inconsistent.
5. **"Who signed this?"** Paper signatures with illegible initials.
   When two staff are required (controlled substance count), enforcing
   the second signature is by trust.
6. **"New owner / new admin."** Records vanish during transitions.
   The state asks for prior-year drill logs and they're "in storage."
7. **"My staff is multilingual but our forms aren't."** Bilingual
   training acknowledgments and bilingual complaint posters are
   common gaps.
8. **"PRN approvals."** PRN psych meds (e.g., as-needed lorazepam)
   need specific documentation rationale that staff often don't
   articulate.
9. **"Resident funds."** Even small balances are a citation trap.
   Most small RCFEs would rather not hold resident funds at all but
   end up holding them anyway.
10. **"Family is the auditor we forget about."** Family complaints to
    the ombudsman trigger surprise visits more often than randomized
    inspection schedules do.

### Failure modes that lead to citations or worse

- Missed medication doses without documented reason.
- TB or fingerprint lapse — staff working uncleared.
- Incident not reported to CCLD inside the required window. `[V]`
- Untrained staff performing a task requiring training.
- Postings missing or illegible.
- Hot water above limit at a single resident fixture. `[V]`
- Choking-risk resident receiving non-modified diet inconsistently.
- Resident found in a posture-support device without an MD order
  on file. `[V]`
- Controlled substance count discrepancy not reconciled.
- Operating over capacity.
- Operating with a special-condition resident (hospice, dementia)
  without the relevant waiver.
- Lapsed liability insurance.
- Admin certificate lapsed while still operating.

### Pre-audit "fire drill" anatomy

When an inspection is anticipated, the admin executes a 12-step
preparation routine (compressed below). The product's value
proposition for audit-readiness is to make this *not a fire drill*:

1. Pull every resident chart and verify the §6 documentation matrix.
2. Re-print missing or stale forms; chase signatures.
3. Re-print MAR cover sheets.
4. Print 6 months of fire drill logs; verify shift coverage met.
5. Print 12 months of temp logs; verify gaps filled with explanations.
6. Verify postings; re-print if torn or moved.
7. Re-confirm staff certifications; chase any missing TB renewals.
8. Confirm current census matches license capacity and waiver
   conditions.
9. Verify emergency disaster plan reflects current names and phone
   numbers.
10. Verify resident trust reconciliation is current.
11. Brief staff on common interview questions.
12. Tour the building as the inspector would.

## 12. Cross-module dependency map (preview for Phase 2)

Operations sits on top of and depends on:

- **Residents** — chart of record; admission/discharge state; level-of-care.
- **Staff** — personnel file; certifications; schedule.
- **Incidents** — LIC 624 lifecycle; state + family notification timestamps.
- **Trackers** — Vitals, ADL, Hygiene, Skin Check, Seizure, Sleep, Toileting (resident-bound); Inventory, Cleaning (facility-bound). These are the *evidence layer* for many audit requests.
- **eMAR** — medication evidence; missed/refused/held documentation; controlled substance count surface.
- **Compliance** — current home for some posting/document tracking; needs expansion vs. the §6 inventory.
- **Calendar** — drill scheduling, inspection scheduling, expiring item alerts.
- **Tasks** — owner-assignable follow-ups for any of the above.
- **CRM** — pre-admission pipeline.
- **Billing** — resident trust and rate changes feed audit needs.
- **Notes** — operational journal; already deep-linkable via
  `#/facility-portal/notes`.

Phase 2 will produce a traceability matrix: for each §6 row and each
§7 document, which existing module owns it, what gap exists, and
whether the resolution is *configuration, integration, or new
capability*.
